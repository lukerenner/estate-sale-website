If you love to shop for art at garage sales or antique shops, how do you know if a piece of art is worth something? Antique Appraiser Gary Germer stopped by to share his expert tips. Understanding terms: Painting = Original, one of a kind, artwork created by an artist. Can be various methods, oil paint, acrylic, watercolor, collage, etc. Artist prints = hand made multiples. with many different styles, but made usually with some sort of printing press or hand done screening, etc. All hands on printing, could be etching, engraving, lithograph, serigraph, mezzotint, etc etc. Mechanically reproduced prints = inexpensive mass produced prints, posters, newsprint (magazines, newspapers). Mainly decorative value only. Tools + you need only one a strong jewelers loupe or other form of magnification at least 30X power. First thing to look for is: if the artwork is made up of little dots. late 19th and early 20th century inexpensive prints were made with a photo-mechanical process, using a three color dot separation. There are variations of this with different methods, but they will have dots. Tiny tiny tiny dots. Sometimes squares. but you can only see them under magnification. If you see these dots, then you do not have anything of significant value. Second thing: Printing plate marks indention on paper, Signature in pencil along the outer edge of the image, production number on the outer edge. indicates a hand pulled print, made by the artist himself and has a limited number made. Making it more likely to be valuable. Some are unknown artist and they are inexpensive. Others great masters can be very valuable. Third, if no dots, Look for brush strokes or pressure marks. make sure the color follows the brush strokes. or if pen or pencil, look for pressure marks. And a signature in the art work and not in the margin. Once you know if you have a painting or a good artist print and not a cheap mechanical print, you can then research the artist, make note of condition and such to determine the value.
