When it's time to clean out your parent's home, knowing where to begin can feel overwhelming. But Gary Germer can help get you started. The antique appraiser joined us to share his expert tips. Cleaning Out Your Parents’ Home: 1. Pre-planning with parents and family: a. Make clear IN WRITING who is going to inherit what. Money, investments, real estate is fairly easy establishing value and dividing, but personal things are much harder. No masking tape with names on it. No "mom told me" only what is in writing counts in the end. b. Parents might hide money or jewelry but need to either tell a close friend or write down where you put things and keep that with your important papers. Millions of dollars in cash and valuables are now in landfills because no one stumbled on them after someone passed. c. Choose 1 person only to be executor 2. Dealing after the fact with an estate: a. First the obvious: distribute willed and gifted items. Throw out wet garbage, old food, old newspapers, obvious garbage. (If in doubt, save it). Secure guns/gold; family photos and papers b. Check for hidden money and valuables; Clothing pockets, Books, button jars, above/behind closet doors, in jars of nails in the garage. etc. (put all jewelry, silver, gold, guns somewhere safe. Those are the most liquid) c. If handling the sale yourself: relax, you are going to screw up but only a little. After the family is done, the remaining in the house is for liquidation. Sell First! Donate second! get things sorted by subject. Set the rest up to look like nice department store, priced clearly, all clean and polished. Hire a professional appraiser or estate provider to come help price things. DO NOT SELL TO THEM... DO NOT SELL TO THEM. Pay them by the hour for their time. 3. When you need professional help: a. When you are emotionally overwhelmed. b. When there are special collections/accumilations c. when there is fighting going on between family members. d. When you are donating a significant amount of property
