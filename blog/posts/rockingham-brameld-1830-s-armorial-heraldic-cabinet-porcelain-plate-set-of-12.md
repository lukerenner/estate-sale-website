Set of 12 William IV, English Heraldic porcelain plates by Rockingham Works Brameld, Circa 1830-1837.

Each with a hand-painted blue heraldic shield with yellow/gold fleurs-de-lis (or similar) set inside a draped, ermine-lined mantle and crown. The rim and border have a vibrant apricot/orange band accented with raised gold floral motifs and white cartouches. Polychrome sprays of pink roses and blue forget-me-nots are interspersed around the white center. Absolutely stunning, I only wish I knew the history of the family.

Because this bears the full Royal Warrant backstamp rather than an unmarked "blank," it was an official, high-tier bespoke commission from the Rockingham factory during its peak artistic period (the same era it produced King William IV's famous state dessert service)

In the 1830s, European aristocracy frequently ordered fine bone china services from premier English factories. The combination of the 1830-1837 date, the ermine-lined peerage mantle, and the 3 natural lilies on blue shield strongly points to a French noble family (most notably Le Fevre d'Ormesson or Anjorrant) ****commissioning an armorial service abroad—or an English family with historic French heraldic quarterings—during the July Monarchy period in France.

There are 12 plates total, they are stacked in the first photo.

Hallmark stamp at back.

All are in excellent condition, no chips, cracks, or repairs.

Each measures 10 3/4"
