A grand piano has remained a timeless staple of both luxury and art. A classic item of bestowment through the generations, garnering nightly standing ovations on the world stage, these valuable instruments have remained a steadfast example of uniting cultures through the bonds of music. Cherished heirlooms that have survived wars and transcontinental migrations, owning a grand piano is a testament to the passion for restoring and preserving an iconic piece of history.

Four years ago, we acquired an estate containing a very rare Hornung & Møller rosewood piano crafted in Copenhagen. Hornung & Møller dominated the piano industry in the mid-1800s, founded by hat-maker Conrad Christian Hornung (1801-1873).

{% blogImage "free-hornung-and-m-ller-piano-01", "The Hornung & Møller grand piano and bench" %}

The Danish piano company introduced iron frames to the world of piano engineering. The instrument is an important specimen in piano history — a rare piece renowned for its ornately decorated rosewood details and unique cast iron frame, partnered with quality Danish carpentry.

{% blogImage "free-hornung-and-m-ller-piano-04", "Detail of the piano's inlaid rosewood cabinet" %}

{% blogImage "free-hornung-and-m-ller-piano-05", "The piano's carved lyre pedal mechanism" %}

{% blogImage "free-hornung-and-m-ller-piano-06", "Close-up of the piano's iron frame and strings" %}

By 1843, the factory achieved the prestigious honor of supplying piano instruments to the royal court. In 1851, Hornung transferred the entire company to his employee Hans Peter Møller, whose name was added to the company's name. At Møller's death in 1859, the company was led by his widow, with his 20-year-old son Frederik Møller as general manager. In 1872, Conrad Christian Hornung (a nephew, b. 1842) emigrated to the United States and expanded the family's piano empire in San Francisco, California. The factories closed in July 1972 after producing more than 50,000 instruments for acclaimed artists, politicians, and celebrities, not to mention holding residency in a number of the world's best concert halls.
