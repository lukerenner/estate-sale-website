If you love shopping garage sales, Antique appraiser Gary Germer joined us to share tips to make your next outing easier and more productive.

**Plan a route.** Go to EstateSale-Finder.com (all local), estatesales.org, and estatesales.net. Also check out Craigslist, OfferUp.com, and Nextdoor.com to find the family garage sales. Sort through what sounds interesting and make a route — choose the one sale that stands out and go there first, then route from there.

**Go early.** Be in line half an hour before they open. This is a case where the early bird gets the worm; if you're not the early bird, remember it's the second rat that gets the cheese.

**Bring the right gear.** A loupe/magnifier, a magnet, a flashlight, a small tape measure, your phone (a.k.a. the oracle), coffee, snacks, wet wipes, blankets, and packing things, so you can see what you're missing.

**Have an idea of what you're looking for** — vintage clothing, fabrics, books, antique picture frames, etc. — and go to those areas first. Not that you won't look at everything, but especially if it's an estate (whole house) sale, you need to have a plan on where to go first.

**Bring one friend.** Garage saling is a ninja sport — in and out and onto the next one. Too many people is just frustrating; have one to drive, the other to navigate.

**Bring cash.** Most garage sales do not take anything but cash. Professional sales will sometimes take cards, but few garage sales will. Bring small bills — no one wants to break a $100.

**Helpful search tool:** go to google.com — in the search bar there's a little camera icon you can use to take a picture of an item, and it will bring up similar items from online for information and for sale.

**Plan for bartering.** Everyone has a technique, but politeness works so much better than being rude. Never gloat afterwards — be grateful, and then gloat in your car if you made a big score. Never offer less than half of the asking price; it's considered rude.
