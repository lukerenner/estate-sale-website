If you love shopping garage sales, Antique appraiser Gary Germer joined us to share tips to make your next outing easier and more productive. Have a route planned: go to EstateSale-Finder.com (all local), estatesales.org, and estatesales .net. Also, check out Craigslist, OfferUp.com and Nextdoor.com to find the family garage sales. Sort through what sounds interesting and make a route. Choose the one sale that stands out and go there first, then route from there. Go early., be in line a 1/2 hour before they open. This is a case where the early bird gets the worm. If you are not the early bird, then remember it is the second rat that gets the cheese. Bring a loupe/magnifier, a magnet, a flashlight, a small tape measure, your phone (aka, the oracle) Coffee, snacks, wet wipes, blankets and packing things. you can see what you are missing. Have an Idea of what you are looking for, i.e. vintage clothing, fabrics, books, antique picture frames, etc. and go to those areas first. not that you will. not look at everything, but especially if it is an estate (whole house) sale, you need to have a plan on where to go first Bring one friend. Garage sale is a ninja sport. In and out and onto the next one. Too many people is just frustrating. one to drive, the other to navigate. Bring CASH. Most garage sales do not take anything but cash. Professional sales will sometimes take cards, but few garage sales will. Bring small bills. NO ONE wants to break a $100 Helpful search tool: go to ww.google.com. In the search bar there is the little camera icon which you can use to take a picture of an item and it will bring up other items like it from online for information and for sale. Plan for bartering. Everyone has a technique. But politeness works so much better than being rude. and Never gloat afterwards, but be grateful and then gloat in your car if you made a big score. NEVER offer less than 1/2 of the asking price, it is considered rude.
